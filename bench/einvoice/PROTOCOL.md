# Protocol — einvoice benchmark, v2 (pre-registered)

Written on 23 September 2026, before any run on a real model, and frozen by the commit that adds it. Any later change is listed under *Deviations*, with its date and reason. Every result is published, favourable or not.

## Question

Does coupling an invoicing agent to the `einvoice` gauge with the yoke change what it ships, and at what cost — judged by graders that are not Keyross? And does it change more when the yoke also knows the order the invoice is issued for (a third arm, added before the full run — see *Deviations*)?

## Design

- **Paired comparison, three arms.** (1) without the yoke; (2) `Yoke(gauge="einvoice")` — the official rules; (3) `Yoke(gauge="einvoice", ctx={"order": order})` — the official rules and the order (deviation 4). The same agent, model, prompt and limits in every arm; the only difference between the arms is the yoke.
- **Tasks.** The 50 purchase orders in `orders/` (generated by `orders.py`, seed 2026, 10 per scenario: domestic, exempt, intra_community, reverse_charge, allowances), committed before the run. Every task is solvable: the 50 reference invoices pass the three judges below.
- **Repetitions.** Each task runs twice in each arm: 300 runs, 100 blocks. A block is (task, repetition); a paired comparison uses the two arms' runs of each block (deviation 4).
- **Agent.** `agent.py`: Deep Agents 0.7.18 on LangChain 1.4.2 / LangGraph 1.2.12; the system prompt and task message in `agent.py`; at most 4 writes of the invoice and 12 model calls per run; recursion limit 60; the `task` tool (subagents) hidden in every arm; a worked example in the system prompt of every arm — see *Deviations*.
- **Model.** `anthropic:claude-haiku-4-5` through `langchain-anthropic` 1.7.3, provider defaults, `max_tokens` 32,000, streaming — amended before any run, see *Deviations*.
- **Treatment.** Arm 2 — `Yoke(gauge="einvoice")`: the CEN EN 16931 artefacts 1.3.16, executed by Keyross after every write; a red write is reverted and only the rule ids are returned. Arm 3 — the same, plus the gauge's delta oracles, with the order in the yoke's context (the harness gives it, not the model): invoice number and currency, every line, the VAT breakdown, the totals, in EN 16931 arithmetic; a red write is reverted and only the categories are returned (`order.lines`, `order.vat`…), never the expected amounts.

## Judges — none of them is Keyross

1. **Validator** — easybill/en16931-validator 0.7.0 (`sha256:e2f84d3d371e95d9eae2da0ccaef5a13bf01f2e58278e9080994ae763d8914dd`), running locally, CEN EN 16931 release 1.3.16: an independent *implementation* of the one official rule set. Any fatal rule makes the invoice invalid.
2. **Schema** — the Factur-X 1.09 EN 16931 XSD, from the factur-x 6.8 wheel (`sha256:02b57dd57f59d0cdd87f034a538bf30a4b9241a72a19cd75fe1538233163ee15`). It checks the structure, which the CEN rules do not.
3. **Order** — `grade.order_mismatches`: number, currency, every line (quantity, price, net amount, VAT category and rate), the VAT breakdown per category and rate, and the totals, against the amounts the order implies (EN 16931 arithmetic, half-up to the cent, tolerance 0.005).

An invoice is **correct** when all three judges accept it. Keyross' own verdict is recorded only to measure its agreement: its official rules against the validator, its delta oracles against the order judge.

In arm 3, the treatment and the order judge check the same property — separate code, one specification (EN 16931 arithmetic, half-up to the cent). A delivered invoice of arm 3 matches the order almost by construction. What arm 3 measures is whether the agent reaches such an invoice from the categories alone, within its limits, and at what cost: an invoice it does not deliver is not correct. The validator and the schema stay independent of every arm; the human review checks the order judge.

## Endpoints

**Primary** — correct delivered invoice, per block. Two paired comparisons are tested: arm 1 against arm 2 (H1, as registered) and arm 1 against arm 3 (H4, deviation 4), each with the exact two-sided McNemar test on the discordant pairs, the two p-values Holm-adjusted, family-wise α = 0.05. Arm 2 against arm 3 is reported the same way, as exploratory, without a test decision. Reported with exact counts and 95 % Wilson intervals.

**Secondary** (descriptive, with intervals, no correction for multiplicity):
- in what ships, per arm: not delivered, fatal rule (validator), schema violation, order mismatch, and *valid but wrong* (passes validator and schema, does not match the order);
- the model alone: first write correct, first write valid, per arm;
- cost: writes and pit stops per run; tokens and seconds per run, with the paired overhead of the yoke (mean, 95 % bootstrap interval, 10,000 resamples, seed 7); yoke check time; estimated cost at list prices;
- agreement between Keyross and the validator on every graded invoice, and between Keyross' delta oracles and the order judge;
- the red flags returned to the agent, by category;
- correct invoices per scenario.

## Hypotheses, declared before the run

- **H1** — the yoke increases the share of correct delivered invoices (primary endpoint).
- **H2** — risk: the yoke may *increase* valid-but-wrong invoices, if the agent bends the order to silence a red flag. Reported whatever its sign.
- **H3** — sanity: first-write rates agree between the arms, since the arms differ only after the first write. A gap points to noise or a harness defect.
- **H4** — the yoke with the official rules and the order increases the share of correct delivered invoices compared with no yoke (primary endpoint; deviation 4).
- **H5** — risk: the order check can refuse a write the official rules accept, so arm 3 may deliver nothing more often than the other arms, within the same limits. Reported whatever its sign (deviation 4).

## Human review

After the run, 21 delivered invoices are drawn with `random.Random(2027)`: 7 per arm among the delivered invoices (deviation 4; 10 per arm when two arms were planned). They are renamed so the arm is hidden (`review.py`). The reviewer — Wassim Amri — decides, for each, whether it is a correct invoice for its order, using the grid in the review sheet. We report the agreement between the human verdict and the automated *correct*, and list every disagreement.

## Pilot

Before the full run: order-01 to order-05 (one per scenario), one repetition, both arms. It measures the real token use and checks the harness. It is excluded from the analysis. If it reveals a harness defect, the defect is fixed, the fix is recorded under *Deviations*, and the protocol is frozen again before the full run. Arm 3 gets its own pilot, the same five orders, one repetition, arm 3 only (deviation 4); it is excluded from the analysis too.

## Errors

A run that ends in an API error is run again once, at the end. If it fails again, its block is excluded from every comparison, and the report counts it.

## What is published

This protocol, the raw results (one JSON line per run), the report, every delivered invoice, and the review sheet with the reviewer's verdicts.

## Deviations

1. **23 September 2026, before any run — model.** `anthropic:claude-sonnet-5` replaced by `anthropic:claude-haiku-4-5`, at the owner's request (cost). No run, pilot included, had been made; nothing else changes. With its provider defaults, Haiku 4.5 answers without extended thinking.
2. **23 September 2026, after the pilot — harness.** The pilot (run `20260923T141730Z`, excluded from the analysis) showed that Deep Agents' default *general-purpose* subagent, reached through the `task` tool, writes files outside the main agent's middleware: outside the yoke, the per-run limits and the token accounting. With the yoke, 2 of the 5 delivered-or-not runs shipped an invoice written this way. Changes, in both arms: the `task` tool is hidden from the model (the benchmark measures writing an invoice, not delegating it); tokens are counted over every model call of the run (a callback) instead of the main thread's messages; `writes` counts executed writes, `write_attempts` includes calls blocked by the limit, `task_calls` must stay 0. Keyross itself was fixed as well: the yoke now measures what a delegated run returns or writes, and keeps a red document out of the agent's files. A second pilot checks the fixed harness before the full run.
3. **23 September 2026, after the pilot — task design (floor effect).** Without help, Haiku 4.5 produced no correct invoice in either arm of the pilot (0/5 and 0/5; the schema was violated in 7 of 7 delivered invoices), so the primary endpoint could not discriminate. At the owner's request, the system prompt of both arms now ends with a worked example: an order that is not among the 50 tasks and the invoice issued for it (`example/`; it passes the three judges) — what a company gives as a template. The tasks, the judges, the endpoints and the analysis are unchanged. The second pilot runs with this prompt.
4. **23 September 2026, after the second pilot, before the full run — a third arm.** The second pilot (run `20260923T151115Z`, excluded from the analysis) showed the dominant error in what ships to be *valid but wrong*: invoices coherent with themselves, accepted by the official rules and the independent validator, that bill the wrong amount — `12.75 × 89.95` written `1146.81` instead of `1146.86` and carried into every total; a VAT amount one cent off, inside the tolerance of BR-CO-17. The official rules cannot see those errors; only the order can. At the owner's request, Keyross adds delta oracles to the `einvoice` gauge (0.2.0), which check an invoice against its order, and the benchmark adds a third arm that yokes the agent to the official rules *and* the order. Arms 1 and 2, the tasks, the prompt, the limits and the judges are unchanged. Changes: 300 runs (50 tasks × 3 arms × 2); two primary comparisons, H1 (arm 1 → arm 2, as registered) and H4 (arm 1 → arm 3), Holm-adjusted — for H1, stricter than registered; arm 2 → arm 3 exploratory; H5 added; a run lost after its retry takes its block out of every comparison; Keyross' delta verdict recorded on every graded invoice, with its agreement with the order judge; the red flags recorded by category; the human review draws 7 invoices per arm (21). The prompt still calls the listed items EN 16931 rules; the order categories (`order.header`, `order.lines`, `order.vat`, `order.totals`) name what differs, and arm 3's pilot checks that the agent acts on them. Stated before the full run: in arm 3, the treatment and the order judge check the same property with separate code, so a delivered invoice of that arm matches the order almost by construction; H4 measures whether the agent reaches such an invoice from the categories alone, within its limits — and H5 whether it gives up instead.
